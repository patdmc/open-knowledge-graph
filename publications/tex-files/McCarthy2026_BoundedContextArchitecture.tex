\begin{abstract}
Any system that accumulates knowledge under bounded active context
faces a fundamental tradeoff between consistency and availability.
We show that this single constraint---bounded context---predicts
four architectural consequences that independently emerge across
software, biological, and organizational systems.

First, a directed graph with typed edges is the cost-optimal
representation: factoring shared structure preserves information
while reducing context load, and sub-linear dependency queries
require stored adjacency. Second, as action repertoire grows,
control architectures converge toward escalation: lower levels
resolve locally, signaling upward only on failure. Third, shared
propositions precipitate from the action space into a separated
knowledge structure---normalization in Codd's sense---eliminating
the update anomalies that manifest as catastrophic forgetting in
architectures without partition boundaries. Fourth, agency
($\gamma > 0$) and learning efficiency ($\eta_M > 0$) are the
minimal structural invariants of persistence under positive novelty
rate; their product $I(E) = \gamma \cdot \eta_M$ is a natural
measure of intelligence as rate of adaptation to novelty.

These results are not independent insights but consequences of one
constraint. Empirical confirmation comes from cancer genomics:
grouping mutations by coupling-channel boundaries (the graph's
partition structure) predicts overall survival with $p < 10^{-14}$
across 71,983 patients, while total mutation count does not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Any system that persists in a physical world is caught between two
forces. From above: entropy is positive, the world produces novel
states, and an entity that does not grow its knowledge $K$ eventually
encounters a state it cannot handle. Growth is not optional; it is
forced by the novelty rate that entropy guarantees. From below:
active context $C_n$ is bounded, and $|K|$ eventually exceeds $C_n$.
The system must structure what it knows or drown in its own
knowledge. These two forces---growth pressure from entropy, capacity
constraint from bounded context---define the problem. Everything in
this paper follows from their intersection.

The constraint admits exactly two responses: minimize the need for
context or maximize context capacity. Strategy~1 keeps $|K|$ small
(fungible units, no hierarchy, partition tolerance through
redundancy). Bacteria, BitTorrent swarms, and transformer attention
layers all follow this pattern: flat, replaceable, and robust by sheer
count. Strategy~2 structures $K$ into addressable hierarchy (typed
edges, scoped domains, sub-linear retrieval). At small scale,
Strategy~1 does not merely suffice---it \emph{outperforms}. When
$|K| \ll C_n$, exhaustive search is faster than indexed retrieval
because the constant dominates the exponent. The dangerous property of
the inferior scaling class is not that it fails but that it succeeds,
conspicuously, until a crossover it cannot see.

\emph{Physarum polycephalum} (slime mold) provides a striking
existence proof that the crossover is real. Under abundant resources,
\emph{Physarum} persists as individual fungible cells (Strategy~1).
Under scarcity, the cells aggregate into a tubular network that solves
shortest-path problems through physical flow dynamics alone
\cite{Nakagaki2000}: parallel exploration followed by topological
optimization. No neuron, no central controller, no designer. The same
cells, the same genome, switching from swarm to graph when the
pressure crosses the threshold. Graph structure emerges from the
constraint, not from design. Large language models are undergoing the
same transition now: retrieval-augmented generation, tool use, and
agent architectures are graph structure aggregating around a flat
substrate under scaling pressure.

Software exhibits this universally. Every system contains directed
adjacency with typed edges across multiple simultaneous substrates:
call graphs, import graphs, type graphs, dataflow graphs, service
graphs, schema graphs, build graphs, and deployment graphs. The graph
property is not an architectural choice but intrinsic to what software
is. The same graphs appear in biological systems: gene regulatory
networks, protein interaction networks, metabolic pathways, neural
connectivity, and the coupling channels that coordinate multicellular
behavior.

This paper shows that one constraint---bounded active context---predicts
four architectural consequences that independently emerge across these
domains:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Graph structure} is cost-optimal for managing consistency
   and availability under bounded context
   (Section~\ref{sec:graph}).
\item \textbf{Escalation} is the cost-optimal control architecture:
   handle locally, escalate on failure
   (Section~\ref{sec:escalation}).
\item \textbf{Normalization} of shared propositions into a separated
   knowledge structure is the cost-optimal consistency mechanism
   (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}).
\item \textbf{Agency and learning efficiency} are the minimal
   structural invariants of persistence under positive novelty rate
   (Section~\ref{sec:intelligence}).
\end{enumerate}

These are not independent insights but consequences of one constraint.
Cancer, viewed from this framing, is the defection of cells from the
graph architecture back to swarm: severing the coupling channels that
carry the topology \cite{McCarthy2026e}. In cancer genomics, grouping
mutations by coupling-channel boundaries---the graph's partition
structure---predicts overall survival with $p < 10^{-14}$ across
71,983 patients, while total mutation count does not
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical}).

Throughout this paper, ``cost-optimal'' means selectively favored under
competition: entities paying higher costs for the same operations lose
resources to competitors paying less. Under sustained competition,
cost-optimal structure is selected for and alternatives are eliminated.
This is selective necessity, not logical impossibility of alternatives.

\section{Formal Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

We introduce the definitions required for the four main results.
Each definition appears once; subsequent sections reference them.

\begin{definition}[Entity, World, Uncertainty]
\label{def:entity}
An entity $E = (K, A, \sigma, \gamma)$ consists of a knowledge base $K$
(propositions about world $W$), an action space $A$ (executable
actions), a selection mechanism $\sigma$ that chooses
actions given context, and an agency parameter $\gamma$. The world state space $W$ is a
measurable space; the true state $w \in W$ is not directly observable.
The \emph{uncertainty} of $E$ about state $w$ given knowledge $K$ is:
\[
U(w, K) = -\log P(w \mid K)
\]
The expected uncertainty is the conditional entropy:
\[
H(W \mid K) = \mathbb{E}_w[U(w, K)] = -\sum_w P(w \mid K) \log P(w \mid K)
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Proposition and Knowledge Base]
\label{def:knowledge}
A \emph{proposition} $p$ is a claim about $W$ with an associated
likelihood function $\ell_p : W \to [0,1]$. A \emph{knowledge base} $K$
is a collection of propositions. The posterior over world states given
$K$ is $P(w \mid K) \propto P_0(w) \cdot L_K(w)$
where $P_0$ is a prior and $L_K$ is the joint likelihood determined by
$K$. Propositions may have arbitrary dependencies; $L_K$ need
not factor as a product of individual likelihoods. The existence of
dependencies is what motivates graph structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Bounded Active Context]
\label{def:context}
The \emph{active context capacity} $C_n$ is bounded: $|\mathrm{ctx}(L_n,
t)| \leq C_n$. For an informed action $a$, the \emph{relevance
density} is:
\[
\rho(a) = \frac{|K^{[a]}|}{|\mathrm{ctx}(t)|}
\]
where $K^{[a]} \subseteq K$ is the knowledge relevant to $a$.
$\rho = 1$ when context holds exactly what $a$ requires;
$\rho \to 0$ when context is dominated by irrelevant content.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Growth and Curation Regimes]
\label{def:regimes}
$K$ has a maximum capacity $K_{max}$. When $|K| < K_{max}$, $K$ is in
the \emph{growth regime}: propositions accumulate freely. When
$|K| = K_{max}$, $K$ is in the \emph{curation regime}: adding a new
proposition requires making space.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Encoding Hierarchy]
\label{def:hierarchy}
An \emph{encoding hierarchy} is a continuous space $E(c,r)$
parameterized by runtime cost $c \geq 0$ and reversibility
$r \in [0,1]$. Interfaces $L_0, L_1, \ldots, L_n$ define discrete
encoding levels with increasing cost and reversibility. $L_0$
(genomic/constitutional) has highest cost of error and lowest
reversibility; $L_n$ (active context) has lowest cost and highest
reversibility. The number of interfaces is substrate-determined.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Agency]
\label{def:agency}
Agency $\gamma \in [0, 1]$ is the probability that the entity engages its
adaptive capacity $M$ in response to a learning signal. An entity with
$\gamma = 0$ responds to no signal. An entity with $\gamma > 0$
engages $M$ on signals, resolves uncertainty, and maintains
$C_{\mathrm{free}} > 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Novelty Rate]
\label{def:novelty}
The \emph{novelty rate} $\nu(E)$ is the rate at which $E$ encounters
world states $w$ where $U(w, K) > 0$:
$\nu(E) = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T}
|\{t \leq T : U(w_t, K(t)) > 0\}|$.
Novelty is a property of the entity--environment coupling, not of
the environment alone. The elimination argument requires that $\nu > 0$
includes eventual survival-relevant novelty.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Free Context]
\label{def:free}
$C_{\mathrm{free}}(t) = C_n - |\mathrm{ctx}_{\mathrm{unresolved}}(t)|$:
active context capacity not consumed by unresolved content. Free context
is the entity's reserve capacity for novel states. The architecture
derived in this paper exists to maximize $C_{\mathrm{free}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Intelligence]
\label{def:intelligence}
For an entity $E$ with agency $\gamma$ and learning efficiency $\eta_M$
(Definition~\ref{def:eta}):
$I(E) = \gamma \cdot \eta_M$.
Intelligence is the rate of adaptation to novelty.
Theorem~\ref{thm:invariants} establishes that $\gamma > 0$ and
$\eta_M > 0$ are the minimal structural invariants of persistence
under positive novelty rate. These are necessary conditions, not
sufficient ones---physical integrity and energy supply are also
required but are not information-theoretic invariants.
\end{definition}

Additional definitions required for specific sections (Factoring,
Information Preservation, Delegation Overhead, Action Space, Higher-Order
Function Space $M$, $M$ Efficiency, Encoding Permanence, Persistence)
are introduced where first needed.

\begin{remark}[Selective Necessity]
\label{rem:necessity}
The results in this paper show that certain structures are
\emph{cost-optimal} under bounded context.
A reviewer may ask: is it \emph{necessary}, or merely sufficient?
In isolation, an entity could use any representation. Under competition
for finite resources, the cost differential becomes a survival
differential. Under sustained competition, cost-optimal structure is
selected for and alternatives are eliminated. Throughout this paper,
``cost-optimal'' carries this selective force: not logical impossibility
of alternatives, but elimination of alternatives under competition.
\end{remark}

\section{Graph Structure Is Cost-Optimal Under Bounded Context}
\label{sec:graph}

Two independent arguments establish graph structure as cost-optimal:
the consistency argument (information preservation requires factoring,
which is graph construction) and the availability argument (sub-linear
maintenance requires stored adjacency).

\subsection{Consistency: Graph from Information Preservation}

\begin{definition}[Factoring]
\label{def:factoring}
Let $p_1, \ldots, p_m \in K$ share mutual information: there exists
$F^*$ such that $\ell_{p_i}(w) = g_i(\ell_{p_i}^{\text{res}}(w), F^*(w))$.
\emph{Factoring} extracts $F^*$ into a single named unit and replaces
the shared component in each $p_i$ with a link to $F^*$. The definition
does not specify direction or typing; these are derived below.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Information Preservation]
\label{def:preservation}
A space-creating operation $f : K \to K'$ is \emph{information-preserving}
if $P(w \mid K') = P(w \mid K)$ for all $w \in W$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Graph Optimality from Information Preservation]
\label{thm:graph-info}
Let $K$ be in the curation regime with bounded $C_n$. The
information-preserving space-creating operations on $K$ converge on
named units with directed, typed references---a directed graph with
typed edges.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Argument.} Any space-creating operation falls into one of five
categories. We show each either produces graph structure or requires it:

\textbf{Option A: Discard.} Without stored adjacency, the entity cannot
determine which propositions are redundant or what depends on what it
discards. This is blind information loss.

\textbf{Option B: Factor.} Extract shared structure $F^*$ into a named
node with typed edges, replacing redundant copies with references.
Space is created without loss. By definition, this is graph
construction.

\textbf{Option C: Reorganize.} Rewrite propositions into a more compact
equivalent. If dependency relationships are preserved, this requires
storing adjacency. If they are not, downstream propositions are silently
broken. Reorganization that extracts shared structure reduces to
factoring (Option~B).

\textbf{Option D: Lossless compression.} Reduces physical footprint but
does not change the active context bound $C_n$. Necessary but does not
solve the structural problem.

\textbf{Option E: Lossy compression.} Without stored adjacency to scope
the damage, lossy compression is blind discarding (Option~A with extra
steps).

Only factoring (B) creates space, preserves the posterior, and solves
the structural problem. Posterior preservation forces \emph{directed}
edges (reconstruction is asymmetric: revising $F^*$ invalidates
dependents, not vice versa) and \emph{typed} edges (the reconstruction
function $g_i$ must be stored to recover $\ell_{p_i}$).

\subsection{Availability: Graph from Retention Dynamics}

\begin{lemma}[Required Maintenance Operations]
\label{lem:ops}
Under bounded $C_n$ with continuous evidence arrival and
corruptibility, $K$ must support: (i)~addition with provenance,
(ii)~contextual retrieval, (iii)~dependency query, and
(iv)~selective removal with cascade.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Graph Structure from Retention Dynamics]
\label{thm:graph-dynamics}
Any representation achieving sub-linear cost on all four maintenance
operations simultaneously encodes directed adjacency. Under bounded
$C_n$, directed adjacency converges to typed edges because untyped
cascade wastes context on irrelevant re-evaluation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Part 1.} Dependency queries are invoked at evidence-arrival
frequency: when $p$ is invalidated, the system must determine what
depends on $p$.

\textbf{Part 2.} Without stored adjacency, each dependency query
requires examining all $n$ propositions: $\Omega(n)$ per query.

\textbf{Part 3.} Cascade amplifies the cost: each level requires
an $\Omega(n)$ scan without adjacency, versus $O(|D_p|)$ with it.

\textbf{Part 4.} Any sub-linear solution must store, for each
proposition, which others depend on it. This is directed adjacency.

\textbf{Part 5.} Typed edges are the convergence point: untyped
cascade cannot distinguish inferential dependents from mere
associates, expanding cascade to $|\mathrm{adj}(p)|$ rather than
$|D_p|$. Under bounded $C_n$, this expansion consumes context needed
for inference. Typed edges restrict cascade to dependency-bearing
edges, keeping cost proportional to $|D_p|$.

Directed adjacency is necessary for sub-linear maintenance (hard lower
bound). Typed edges are what directed adjacency converges to under
bounded-context pressure (selective necessity per
Remark~\ref{rem:necessity}).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Edge Growth Dominates Node Growth]
\label{cor:edge-growth}
As $K$ matures, the ratio of edges to nodes increases. Each new
proposition arrives with at least one edge (its evidential ground).
Cross-grounding adds edges between existing nodes. A mature knowledge
base is edge-dense: most of its informational value is in the
relationships, not the propositions themselves.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Continuous-weight mechanisms converge toward stored adjacency}

Full self-attention computes $O(n^2)$ pairwise relevances per query---the
cost of recomputing the graph from scratch every step. Sparse attention,
KV caches, and retrieval augmentation progressively store adjacency
explicitly, reducing retrieval to $O(d)$. This trajectory---from dynamic
recomputation toward stored structure---is the cost prediction in action.
Catastrophic forgetting \cite{McCloskey1989,Kirkpatrick2017} is the
empirical confirmation: systems without stored adjacency cannot scope
updates and exhibit the uncontrolled interference the theorem predicts.

\section{Convergence to Escalation Under Bounded Context}
\label{sec:escalation}

\begin{definition}[Delegation Overhead]
\label{def:delegation}
Under top-down allocation, $L_n$ holds state for each managed
subprocess: the subproblem specification, the expected return signal,
and the integration context. Let $\alpha > 0$ be the minimum context
cost per delegation.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Convergence to Escalation at Scale]
\label{thm:topdown}
Under top-down allocation with $k$ concurrent delegations, the
relevance density for a novel problem is:
\[
\rho(a_{\mathrm{novel}}) =
\frac{|K^{[a_{\mathrm{novel}}]}|}{|K^{[a_{\mathrm{novel}}]}| + k \cdot \alpha}
\]
As $|A| \to \infty$ under bounded $C_n$: (i)~$\rho \to 0$;
(ii)~recovery requires shedding supervisory tracking;
(iii)~the shed actions operate under escalation. Let $\phi$ be the
escalation fraction. Since at most $k^* = C_n / (2\alpha)$ actions
can be under top-down supervision:
\[
\phi \geq 1 - \frac{k^*}{|A|} \to 1 \quad \text{as } |A| \to \infty
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each delegation consumes $\alpha$ context slots. When
$k \cdot \alpha \geq C_n / 2$, delegation overhead consumes the
majority of $C_n$. Under bounded $C_n$, the only recovery is to stop
tracking actions that lower levels can handle. Those actions are,
by definition, operating under escalation: handled at the lowest
sufficient level, with upward signaling only on failure. As $|A|$
grows, the fraction under escalation approaches unity.

\textbf{The crossover.} For biological working memory ($C_n \approx 4$,
$\alpha \geq 1$): $k^* = 2$ concurrent delegations. A human supervisor
can actively track 2--3 concurrent delegations before reasoning
quality degrades.
\end{proof}

\textbf{One-directionality.} The convergence is irreversible. Top-down
holds \emph{anticipatory context} (waiting for results); escalation
holds \emph{reactive context} (responding to failures). Anticipatory
context scales with delegations; reactive context scales with failure
rate. As lower levels improve, failure rate decreases, so escalation
overhead \emph{decreases with capability}. Top-down overhead
\emph{increases with capability}. Hierarchical decomposition is not an
alternative to escalation but the intermediate regime through which
top-down converges toward it.

\textbf{Illustration.} A multicellular organism with $10^{13}$ cells
cannot manage each cell through a central controller. Biology's solution
is a hybrid: local autonomy (contact inhibition, growth factor
response) combined with broadcast signals (hormonal regulation, immune
surveillance). Broadcasting is $O(1)$ in delegation context. When
local signaling fails---a cell ignores growth suppression---the immune
system engages: escalation on failure.

\section{Knowledge as Normalization}
\label{sec:normalization}

When multiple action policies share propositions and active context is
bounded, storing each policy's propositions independently produces
$O(n \cdot k)$ context load. Knowledge precipitates from the action
space the same way normal forms precipitate from redundant relations in
Codd's relational model \cite{Codd1970}.

\begin{definition}[Context Load]
\label{def:load}
For entity $E$ with action space $A$ and knowledge representation
$\mathcal{R}$, the \emph{context load} $L(\mathcal{R}, A)$ is the
number of proposition slots required to simultaneously evaluate any
pair of action policies.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Normalization of Shared Propositions]
\label{thm:normalization}
Let $E$ have $n$ knowledge-grounded action policies, average
proposition count $k$ per policy, and overlap fraction $\beta > 0$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(N1)] Under implicit-$K$ (each policy carries its own
   propositions), context load is $O(n \cdot k)$ and total storage
   is $n \cdot k$.
\item[(N2)] Extracting shared propositions into a separated $K$ with
   graph-edge references produces context load
   $|K^{[a_1]} \cup K^{[a_2]}|$ and total storage $|K| + n(1-\beta)k$.
\item[(N3)] $L_{\text{norm}} \leq L_{\text{impl}}$ with strict
   inequality when $\beta > 0$. In the limit $\beta \to 1$,
   per-policy marginal cost approaches $O(1)$.
\item[(N4)] Updating a shared proposition under implicit-$K$ requires
   modifying $n$ copies (Codd's update anomaly). Under normalized $K$,
   updating is $O(1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Parts N1--N3 follow from counting context slots: shared propositions
loaded once under normalization versus $n$ copies under implicit-$K$.
Part N4 is Codd's update anomaly applied to propositions: redundant
copies diverge upon partial update, producing inconsistent action
policies. Normalized $K$ stores each proposition once; all referencing
policies see updates immediately via graph edges.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Retention Criterion]
\label{cor:retention}
Retention is asymmetric: retain when benefit is uncertain; prune only
when benefit is certainly zero. The cost of wrongly pruning
($C_{\text{absent}}$, potentially lethal) dominates the cost of wrongly
retaining ($c_{\text{retain}}$, bounded). In the curation regime,
displacement by marginal value comparison replaces pruning.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[$K/A$ Inseparability]
\label{cor:inseparability}
The content of $K$ is constitutively determined by $A$. Entities with
different action spaces maintain different knowledge graphs. Systems
may implement $K$ and $A$ as distinct modules but cannot optimize them
independently.
\end{corollary}

\textbf{Empirical signature.} Architectures that distribute propositions
across parameters without addressable partition boundaries cannot scope
updates to individual propositions. The empirical signature is
catastrophic forgetting \cite{McCloskey1989,Kirkpatrick2017}: updating a
neural network on new information degrades previously learned
information because the weight update is unscoped. Every proposed
mitigation---elastic weight consolidation, experience replay,
progressive networks---is an attempt to bolt partition boundaries onto
a system that does not natively have them.

\section{What Persists: Structural Invariants and Intelligence}
\label{sec:intelligence}

Sections~\ref{sec:graph}--\ref{sec:normalization} derive what the
architecture looks like. This section asks: what does the architecture
\emph{select for}? What persists, and why doesn't everything converge
on the same architecture?

\subsection{Why not everything}

The architecture derived above is cost-optimal at scale. But one size
does not fit all. A wood-frame house does not need a steel superstructure.
A bacterium does not need BRCA1. A shell script does not need a
service mesh. Below the crossover (Section~\ref{sec:intro}), the flat
architecture is not merely adequate---it is \emph{better}. At small
$n$, $O(2^n)$ with a tiny constant beats $O(n)$ with a big constant.
Exhaustive search outperforms indexed retrieval when there are three
items. The overhead of graph structure, escalation, and normalization
is real, and at small scale it costs more than it saves.

This is why evolution does not produce the architecture everywhere.
Evolution is a local optimizer. Every mutation is evaluated at the
scale it occurs---this cell, this generation, this environment.
A mutation that drops coordination overhead and lets a cell divide
faster wins locally. That is cancer. A mutation that adds a
coordination layer and slows the cell down loses locally---it wins
only at the scale of the organism, over generations, through
selection on the whole.

The architecture emerges not from local optimization but from
selection pressure at a higher scale: the organism that has the
architecture outcompetes the organism that does not, even though
every cell inside the organism would ``prefer'' to drop it. The
graph is a collective solution to a collective problem, and every
component has an incentive to defect from it. The constraint that
forces the architecture also forces the failure mode. The
architecture and the cancer are consequences of the same constraint.

\subsection{Encoding Permanence}

\begin{definition}[Encoding Permanence]
\label{def:encoding}
Let encoding levels $L_0, \ldots, L_n$ have failure costs
$C_0 > \cdots > C_n$ and $\varepsilon$ be the maximum acceptable
expected error cost. The certainty threshold for encoding at $L_i$ is:
\[
\theta_i = 1 - \frac{\varepsilon}{C_i}
\]
\end{definition}

This is arithmetic, not a deep result. The substantive content is the
identification: cost-of-failure determines the certainty threshold.
At $L_0$ (genomic), $C_0$ is lineage extinction, so
$\theta_0 \approx 1$. At $L_n$ (active context), $C_n$ is small, so
$\theta_n$ is meaningfully less than~$1$.

The learning rate at level $L_i$ follows from Hoeffding's inequality
\cite{Hoeffding1963}:
$n_i \geq \log(2 C_i / \varepsilon) / (2\delta^2)$,
giving $\eta_i \propto \delta^2 / \log(C_i / \varepsilon)$.
Since $\theta_0 \gg \theta_n$:
$\eta_{\mathrm{evo}} \ll \eta_{\mathrm{ind}}$.
The learning rate difference between evolution and individual learning
is derived from cost-of-failure structure, not assumed.

\subsection{What persists}

\begin{definition}[Persistence]
\label{def:persistence}
Entity $E$ \emph{persists} if there is no finite $T$ at which
$U(w_T, K(T)) > U_{\mathrm{lethal}}$ terminates it.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$M$ Efficiency]
\label{def:eta}
For learning event $(A', K') = M((A, K), L)$, the learning efficiency
$\eta_M$ is the expected improvement in information gain per unit of
learning signal, conditional on engagement. $\eta_M > 0$: the entity
learns. $\eta_M \leq 0$ persistently: the entity does not persist.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Structural Invariants of Persistence]
\label{thm:invariants}
For any entity persisting under bounded $C_n$ with $\nu(E) > 0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\gamma > 0$ necessarily (free-context exhaustion:
   $\gamma = 0$ entities cannot resolve novel states, so
   $C_{\mathrm{free}} \to 0$).
\item[(ii)] $\eta_M > 0$ necessarily in expectation (without it,
   context fills without relief).
\item[(iii)] Every other information-theoretic property can equal zero
   for some persistent entity.
\end{enumerate}
These are necessary conditions for persistence, not sufficient
ones---physical integrity, energy, and substrate constraints are also
required but are not information-theoretic invariants.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{The intelligence measure and its limitations.}
$I(E) = \gamma \cdot \eta_M$ is a natural first-order measure: the
expected rate of adaptation to novelty, by the law of total expectation.
The identification---intelligence is rate of adaptation, not level of
current performance---is the substantive claim.

However, the raw product is incomplete. It does not account for:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Integration cost.} Learning is harder the more you know.
   Each new proposition must be integrated into a larger, denser graph.
   A system that maintains constant $\eta_M$ against increasing graph
   density is effectively accelerating. The measure should be weighted
   by $|K|$ or graph density.
\item \textbf{Signal efficiency.} Two systems with the same $\eta_M$
   may be processing vastly different quality inputs. A system
   extracting signal from noisy, contradictory data is doing harder work
   than one learning from curated inputs. The measure does not
   distinguish these.
\end{itemize}
A refined measure would weight $\eta_M$ by integration difficulty and
signal quality. The present paper establishes the necessary conditions
($\gamma > 0$, $\eta_M > 0$) and the first-order product. The
refinement is future work.

When $\nu(E) = 0$, the entity faces no novel states and $\gamma = 0$
is viable. Intelligence is what positive novelty rate selects for.
Entities at $\nu = 0$ persist without it.

% SECTION 7: EMPIRICAL CONFIRMATION
% ==================================================================
\section{Empirical Confirmation: Cancer as Architecture Failure}
\label{sec:empirical}

The architecture derived above---graph structure, escalation,
normalization---makes a testable prediction: if biological systems
exhibit this architecture, then measuring the architecture should
predict outcomes better than measuring raw damage.

\textbf{Channel count predicts survival; mutation count does not.}
In cancer genomics, 122 genes map to 8 coupling channels (DNA damage
response, cell cycle, growth signaling, endocrine, immune,
tissue architecture, chromatin remodeling, DNA methylation). Across
71,983 patients in three independent MSK-IMPACT cohorts, the number
of disrupted coupling channels predicts overall survival with
$p < 10^{-14}$ (Cox proportional hazards). Total mutation count does
not. In multivariate analysis controlling for mutation count, age,
sex, and cancer type, channel count remains significant
($\text{HR} = 1.07$, $p < 10^{-25}$) while mutation count is not
\cite{McCarthy2026e}.

This is the architecture's prediction confirmed: the graph's partition
boundaries (channels) carry the signal that the flat count (TMB) does
not. The number of breached boundaries predicts cascade failure---the
same result Theorem~\ref{thm:graph-dynamics} derives for any
knowledge system under bounded context.

\textbf{Channels map to architectural tiers.}
The 8 channels organize into 4 tiers (cell, tissue, organ, organism),
each with a bottom-up channel (local: repair, chromatin, growth
signaling, immune) and a top-down channel (systemic: cell cycle,
methylation, tissue architecture, endocrine) \cite{McCarthy2026f}.
This is the escalation hierarchy (Section~\ref{sec:escalation})
instantiated in biology.

\textbf{Evolutionary confirmation.}
Ortholog analysis of 122 cancer-related genes across 10 species
spanning 1 billion years reveals three evolutionary epochs: a yeast
foundation (20 genes, 1B years), an invertebrate alarm system
(+40 genes, 600M), and a vertebrate refactor (+57 genes, 450M years).
The top-down channels received far more vertebrate expansion
(Immune: 70\% of genes arriving at the fish branch point) than
bottom-up channels (ChromatinRemodel: 12\%). The command side
scaled with organism complexity; the query side was already built.
This is the escalation architecture emerging from evolutionary data
without being imposed.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The four results are consequences of one constraint: bounded context
forces the consistency-availability tradeoff, and every architectural
decision follows from how that tradeoff is managed.

\textbf{The unification.}
The contribution is not any individual result---graph theorists know
graphs are useful, database designers know normalization works,
organizational theorists know escalation scales. The contribution
is showing that these are all consequences of the same constraint.
The architecture is the same across domains because the constraint
is the same. The architecture is also constantly under threat from
local defection (Section~\ref{sec:intelligence}), because the
constraint that forces the global solution also makes the local
optimum look better at every individual step.

\subsection{Predictions}

The central prediction: at significant scaling events in system
size, we will see architectural factorization---redundancy expansion,
additional control layers, new partition boundaries. This prediction
is testable in biology, software, and organizations.

\textbf{Biological prediction.}
At each major jump in organism cell count, the genome should show:
(a)~gene duplication for redundancy (server pooling),
(b)~new coordination genes inserted between existing layers
(middleware), and (c)~new error-handling genes (brakes and
checkpoints). Ortholog analysis of 122 cancer-related genes across
10 species confirms this pattern across three epochs:

\begin{itemize}
\item Yeast ($\sim 1$ cell, 1B years): 20 foundational genes.
\item Fly/worm ($\sim 50{,}000$ cells, 600M years): +40 genes,
   primarily alarm systems and epigenetic machinery.
\item Fish ($\sim 10^{10}$ cells, 450M years): +57 genes arriving
   simultaneously---server pools (AKT$\times 3$, FGFR$\times 3$,
   HLA$\times 3$), the full FANC coordination complex, the immune
   system. A major version release.
\item Mammal ($\sim 10^{12}$ cells, 180M years): +2 genes---CDKN2B
   (cell cycle brake) and CD274/PD-L1 (immune checkpoint). Resilience
   infrastructure, not new capability.
\item Elephant ($\sim 10^{15}$ cells): 20 copies of TP53. Server
   pooling for the single point of failure.
\end{itemize}

The top-down channels (Immune: 70\% arriving at the fish branch point)
received far more vertebrate expansion than the bottom-up channels
(ChromatinRemodel: 12\%). The command side scaled with organism
complexity; the query side was already built. This pattern---new
coordination and control layers scaling with complexity while the
workers remain stable---is exactly what the escalation result
(Section~\ref{sec:escalation}) predicts.

The prediction extends forward: at scaling boundaries not yet examined
(colonial organisms, eusocial insects, multicellular plants), the same
patterns should appear. This is testable with the same ortholog
databases used here.

\textbf{Software prediction.}
As AI systems scale, they will increasingly store adjacency explicitly
(sparse attention, KV caches, retrieval augmentation), adopt
escalation architectures (tool use, agent hierarchies), and
normalize shared knowledge into separated structures (knowledge
graphs with provenance). Each optimization reduces the $O(n^2)$
cost of recomputing structure dynamically to the $O(d)$ cost of
stored adjacency.

\textbf{The encoding permanence threshold.}
The cleanest standalone result is
$\theta_i = 1 - \varepsilon / C_i$
(Definition~\ref{def:encoding}): cost-of-failure determines the
certainty threshold for encoding at each level. This derives the
learning rate hierarchy---why evolution is slow and individual
learning is fast---from one line of arithmetic. The same equation
predicts that organisms with higher cost-of-failure at the genomic
level (longer-lived species) should have slower evolutionary rates,
a prediction consistent with molecular clock data.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}

The results identify information-theoretic invariants of persistence,
not a complete list of everything needed to survive. Physical
integrity, energy supply, and substrate constraints are also required.
The claim is that within the information-theoretic frame, bounded
context is the single constraint that predicts the architecture.

The architecture is cost-optimal, not necessary in the logical sense.
Alternative architectures are possible and sometimes preferable
at small scale. The claim is about convergence under scaling
pressure, not impossibility of alternatives.

The intelligence measure $I(E) = \gamma \cdot \eta_M$ is a first-order
approximation that does not account for integration cost or signal
efficiency. A refined measure is future work.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{The CAP theorem} \cite{Brewer2000, Gilbert2002} establishes
that distributed systems cannot simultaneously guarantee consistency,
availability, and partition tolerance. The present paper applies
the analogous tradeoff to any knowledge system under bounded context:
$C_n$ is the partition.

\textbf{Bounded rationality} \cite{Simon1955} establishes that
cognitive agents satisfice rather than optimize. Our $C_n$ formalizes
a specific consequence: when the bound is hard, structure becomes the
cost-optimal response.

\textbf{Knowledge graphs and description logics}
\cite{Hogan2021,Pearl1988,BaaderCalvaneseEtAl2003,BernersLee2001}
demonstrate that graph structure is effective. The present paper argues
it is cost-optimal: the question is not whether graphs work but why
they independently emerge.

\textbf{Codd's relational model} \cite{Codd1970} established
normalization as the solution to update anomalies. Our normalization
theorem derives the same operation from bounded context rather than
from database design principles.

\textbf{Ashby's law of requisite variety} \cite{Ashby1956} states
that a controller must have at least as many states as the system it
controls. Our escalation result addresses what happens when the
controller's capacity ($C_n$) is bounded below the system's variety.

\textbf{The free energy principle} \cite{Friston2010} derives
minimization behavior from self-organization. Our framework is
complementary: we derive the architecture under which minimization
operates, and distinguish $\gamma = 0$ (no adaptation) from
$\gamma > 0$ (active adaptation).

\textbf{Intelligence measures.}
Hutter \cite{Hutter2005} defines intelligence as average performance;
we define it as rate of improvement. Chollet \cite{Chollet2019}
measures skill-acquisition efficiency, close to our $\eta_M$ but
treated as a benchmark metric rather than derived from persistence
conditions. Schmidhuber \cite{Schmidhuber2010} identifies curiosity
as compression progress but treats it as a design objective rather
than a necessary condition.

\textbf{Catastrophic forgetting}
\cite{McCloskey1989,Kirkpatrick2017} is the empirical signature
predicted by our consistency argument: systems without stored
adjacency and partition boundaries cannot scope updates.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

One constraint---bounded active context---predicts four architectural
consequences: graph structure for consistency and availability,
escalation for control, normalization for knowledge-action consistency,
and agency with learning efficiency as the minimal invariants of
persistence. These are not design preferences but cost-optimal
responses to the tradeoff that bounded context imposes.

The architecture is the same across domains because the constraint is
the same. It does not emerge everywhere because local optimization
favors simpler alternatives at small scale. The architecture emerges
from selection at higher scales---the organism, the organization, the
ecosystem---where the cost of not having it exceeds the cost of
maintaining it. Cancer, organizational dysfunction, and AI
hallucination are all instances of local defection from an
architecture that is globally optimal but locally expensive.

Empirical confirmation comes from cancer genomics (71,983 patients:
channel count predicts survival, mutation count does not) and from
evolutionary data (three scaling epochs, each producing the
architectural patterns the theory predicts). The encoding permanence
threshold $\theta_i = 1 - \varepsilon / C_i$ bridges information
theory and evolutionary biology, deriving the learning rate hierarchy
from cost-of-failure rather than assumption.

The practical consequence: as AI systems scale under bounded context,
they will converge on the same architecture biology and software
independently discovered. The question is not whether they will
converge but whether they will converge by design or by the same
costly trial and error that took biology a billion years.
